\subsection{Representation invariance to synthetic noise}
\label{sec:representation_invariance}

We examine how synthetic noise affects learned representations and whether
SEANA improves their stability while retaining image discrimination. We analyze
ResNet-18 backbones pretrained with BYOL, SimCLR, and VICReg under the baseline,
NSP-denoising, and SEANA training procedures.

\paragraph{Evaluation protocol.}
For each checkpoint, we use $N=2{,}000$ clean CIFAR-10 test images and draw
$K=8$ independent corruptions of each image under the corresponding synthetic
noise condition. Let
$z_{ir}=f_\theta(\mathcal{C}_c(x_i,\xi_{ir}))\in\mathbb{R}^{512}$
denote the globally pooled backbone representation of image $i$ under draw $r$
of corruption condition $c$. Corrupted pixels are clipped to $[0,1]$, and
$f_\theta$ includes the fixed input normalization used by the evaluation code.
All measurements are forward passes with the backbone in evaluation mode.
We use neither class labels nor a fitted probe. No spatial or color augmentation,
denoising, or SEANA resampling is applied during this evaluation. Consequently,
NSP and SEANA identify how each backbone was trained, and the analysis measures
the resulting backbone's response to noisy inputs.

\paragraph{Noise-driven variance fraction.}
We perform a one-way ANOVA variance decomposition grouped by clean-image
identity, pooling squared deviations across feature coordinates. Define
\begin{equation}
    \bar z_i = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{r=1}^{K}z_{ir},
    \qquad
    \bar z = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bar z_i.
\end{equation}
The within-image and between-image dispersions are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    S_W &= \frac{1}{NK}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{r=1}^{K}
             \lVert z_{ir}-\bar z_i\rVert_2^2,\\
    S_B &= \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
             \lVert\bar z_i-\bar z\rVert_2^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here, $S_W$ measures variation induced by changing the corruption while holding
image identity fixed. $S_B$ measures variation between the per-image mean
representations. Because these means use finitely many draws, $S_B$ can still
contain residual noise variation.
Our primary metric is
\begin{equation}
    \nu = \frac{S_W}{S_W+S_B} = 1-\eta^2,
    \qquad
    \eta^2 = \frac{S_B}{S_W+S_B}.
    \label{eq:noise_variance_fraction}
\end{equation}
This is the complement of the ANOVA explained-variance fraction, since
$\mathrm{SS}_W=NK S_W$ and $\mathrm{SS}_B=NK S_B$.
Lower $\nu$ indicates less noise-induced movement relative to total feature
dispersion. The features are not $\ell_2$-normalized for this decomposition,
although $\nu$ is invariant to a common nonzero rescaling of the features.
We use the decomposition descriptively, without an ANOVA significance test.

\paragraph{Image discrimination and effective dimensionality.}
We complement $\nu$ with two diagnostics. For \emph{content re-identification},
we independently corrupt the same $M=500$ images to form query and gallery
sets, then measure top-1 retrieval of the same clean-image identity using cosine
similarity. Chance accuracy is $1/M=0.002$.
For \emph{entropy effective rank}, let $\lambda_j$ be the eigenvalues of
\begin{equation}
    C = \frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
        (\bar z_i-\bar z)(\bar z_i-\bar z)^\top.
\end{equation}
We report
\begin{equation}
    r_{\mathrm{eff}} = \exp\!\left(-\sum_j p_j\log p_j\right),
    \qquad p_j = \frac{\lambda_j}{\sum_\ell\lambda_\ell},
    \label{eq:entropy_effective_rank}
\end{equation}
with $0\log 0=0$.
% Numerically, the implementation omits terms with p_j <= 1e-12.
This measures the dimensionality of the covariance of the per-image means.
Together, rank and retrieval help distinguish stable, discriminative
representations from representations with little variation across images.

\paragraph{Results across noise conditions.}
SEANA lowers the mean noise-driven variance fraction in all six conditions
(Table~\ref{tab:representation_noise_fraction}), with relative reductions of
$23.3$--$45.1\%$. In the high-noise conditions, the baseline fraction ranges
from $5.1\%$ to $11.5\%$, and SEANA reduces it by $29.9$--$45.1\%$ relative
to baseline. These measurements show reduced sensitivity to corruption in
the learned representations.

\paragraph{Invariance alongside image discrimination.}
Under high Gaussian noise (Table~\ref{tab:representation_gaussian_high}),
NSP-trained encoders have the smallest within-image dispersion, but also
substantially smaller between-image dispersion, lower effective rank, and
poorer content retrieval. Their mean noise fraction is consequently higher
than baseline ($0.206$ versus $0.115$). SEANA combines a lower noise fraction
($0.063$) with higher content retrieval ($0.905$ versus $0.786$ for baseline)
and a substantially higher effective rank than NSP ($24.5$ versus $7.1$).
Absolute $S_W$ and $S_B$ depend on feature scale, so we interpret their magnitudes
alongside the scale-invariant fraction, rank, and retrieval metrics. The joint
pattern is consistent with SEANA improving noise invariance while retaining
image discrimination. These descriptive measurements do not establish that
denoising itself causes representational collapse.

\paragraph{Sensitivity to checkpoint membership.}
The averages above use all available checkpoints, whose counts vary across
conditions and training procedures. Restricting comparisons to shared
SSL-method/training-seed pairs preserves the trends. For high Poisson--Gaussian
noise, the matched baseline and SEANA fractions are $0.0788$ and $0.0509$,
respectively, a $35.4\%$ reduction. For high Gaussian noise, using seed 0 for
all three training procedures gives fractions of $0.1147$, $0.2062$, and
$0.0632$ for baseline, NSP, and SEANA, with content retrieval of $0.787$,
$0.247$, and $0.900$, respectively.
